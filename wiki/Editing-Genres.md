# Editing genres

Genres are your own: a tree of genres per library, named as you like, with aliases for the other names a genre goes by. Albums, recordings and performers each carry their own genres, in order. Open a library and choose **Genres**.

## The genre list

The list reads as the tree: each genre follows the genre it sits under, siblings in alphabetical order, and a subgenre shows the path above it ("Under Jazz"). Search matches a genre's name or any of its aliases, ignoring case, accents and punctuation; results show how many subgenres and uses each genre has, 25 to a page.

**New top-level genre** creates a genre under no other. Subgenres are created from their parent's page.

## A genre's page

- **Details**: the name and notes. Two genres under the same parent cannot share a name, and neither can two top-level genres; genres under different parents can.
- **Aliases**: other names that resolve to this genre, such as the name Apple Music uses ("Hip-Hop/Rap") or a spelling. An alias belongs to one genre in the library. When Apple Music imports arrive, each Apple genre name is looked up among the aliases first, then among genre names that are unique in the library.
- **Place in the tree**: where the genre sits. To move it, find the genre to move it under, choose it, and choose **Move**; **Top level, under no genre** takes it out of the tree's branches. A genre cannot go under itself or under one of its own subgenres; that move is refused.
- **Subgenres**: the genres directly under this one, and **Add subgenre** for a new one.
- **Albums**, **Recordings** and **Performers with this genre**: everything that carries the genre.

The links above the genre's name lead to the genres it sits under.

**Delete genre** asks for confirmation. A genre with subgenres, or carried by any album, recording or performer, cannot be deleted; move its subgenres and take it off what carries it first. Its aliases go with it.

## Giving genres to albums, recordings and performers

The **Genres** panel on an album's, recording's or performer's page lists its genres in order; the first is its **main genre**. Find a genre, choose it and choose **Add genre**; **Move up**, **Move down** and **Remove** change the order. Each change is saved at once.

Genres on an album describe the album, genres on a recording the recording, and genres on a performer the performer's work in general. None is copied from another: a jazz album can hold a recording you file under blues.
